Jacket Privacy Policy

Effective date: 10 November 2026

Jacket is a free, open-source e-reader for Android, developed at github.com/friendlyglobal/jacket. This policy explains what happens to your information when you use it.

Summary

Jacket does not collect or transmit any personal data. It uses the internet for one thing only: downloading a natural voice from GitHub, once, after you agree (see Downloading a voice). Nothing is uploaded. The books you open in Jacket cannot connect to the network. There are no analytics, no ads and no accounts. Jacket sends the developer nothing.

What Jacket stores on your device

  • Books. When you import a book (EPUB, PDF, CBZ or TXT) with Add books, Open with or Share, Jacket copies the file into its private storage on your device, along with a cover image that it extracts or generates. Text you share to Jacket is saved as a book the same way.
  • Your reading data. Reading positions, bookmarks, highlights, notes and settings are stored on your device.

Android keeps this storage private to Jacket; other apps cannot read it.

Read-aloud

Jacket can read aloud with two kinds of voice.

  • Natural voices run inside Jacket, on your phone. The text they read is turned into sound on the device and is not passed to any other app or sent anywhere. The voices themselves are downloaded once, when you agree (see below).
  • Your phone’s voice is the text-to-speech engine installed on your phone, such as Google Speech Services. That engine is a separate app with its own privacy policy. When you read aloud with it, Jacket passes the engine the text being read. How the engine handles that text is covered by its own privacy policy.

You choose the voice in Jacket’s Settings or on the player. The natural voices read the languages they know; for any other language, the phone’s voice is used.

Downloading a voice

The natural voices are not inside the app. The first time you listen to a book whose voice is not on your phone, Jacket asks whether to download it. Settings › Voice can also download or remove each set of voices.

  • Only when you agree. Nothing is downloaded until you choose Download. If you choose your phone’s voice instead, Jacket does not ask again. On mobile data, Jacket tells you the size and asks before it starts. When you are not looking at Jacket (listening from the notification, headphones or a car), it never downloads anything new and uses a voice already on your phone. A download you started and that was interrupted (for example because Android closed Jacket) carries on the next time you open Jacket, over Wi-Fi.
  • Where from. The voice files come from Jacket’s own release on GitHub (github.com/friendlyglobal/jacket, release “voices-1”), served by GitHub. Jacket connects to GitHub over an encrypted connection (HTTPS) and to no other place.
  • What GitHub sees. Like any download, GitHub sees your IP address, and the file asked for. Jacket sends no account, no identifier, no cookie and nothing about you, your phone or your books; it does not even send the usual description of the phone that apps send by default. GitHub handles the request under its own privacy statement.
  • Nothing is uploaded. The download only receives files. The text you read never leaves the device.
  • Checked before use. Each file is checked against a size and fingerprint (SHA-256) built into Jacket. A file that doesn’t match is deleted, never used.
  • Stored on your phone, outside backups. Downloaded voices are kept in Jacket’s private storage, in a place that Android’s device backup and phone-to-phone transfer leave out, and they are not part of backup files you export. They take about 145 MB (31 languages) or 32 MB (the Brazilian voice). Settings › Voice removes them; uninstalling Jacket deletes them.

In the source code, this download is one file, voice/VoiceDownload.kt, and an automatic test fails the build if any other part of Jacket opens a network connection.

Backups

  • Android device backup. If you have device backup turned on, Android’s Auto Backup may include Jacket’s library list, reading positions, bookmarks, highlights, notes and settings in your Google account backup. Book files are not included. Google stores this backup under its own terms; the developer has no access to it. You can turn backup off in your phone’s settings.
  • Moving to a new phone. If you use Android’s phone-to-phone transfer, Android may copy Jacket’s data, including your books, directly to the new phone.
  • Backup files you export. You can export a backup file from Jacket yourself. It is saved wherever you choose, such as your phone’s storage or a cloud storage app, and is then handled by that location.

What people near you can see

  • The outer-screen cover and the cover-screen widget show the current book’s cover and title on your phone’s outer screen, where people nearby can see them. You can turn off the outer-screen cover in Jacket’s Settings, and remove the widget from your cover screen or home screen at any time.
  • While read-aloud is playing, Android shows the book’s title in the media notification and on the lock screen.

Permissions

Jacket has Android’s internet permission (“have full network access”) for one use only: downloading a natural voice, as described above. It also uses “view network connections” to know whether the phone is online, and whether it is on mobile data so it can ask first; that permission cannot connect to anything. Jacket does not request the location, contacts, camera, microphone or storage permissions. You choose files through Android’s own file picker. Jacket uses a foreground service so that read-aloud can keep playing with the screen off. Android libraries that Jacket uses for its widget also declare permissions to schedule widget updates.

The books you open cannot use the internet permission: the pages of a book are shown with their network access blocked, and a book’s own scripts cannot open connections.

Google Play

If you install Jacket from Google Play, Google gives developers aggregate statistics such as install counts, and crash reports from people who have chosen to share diagnostics with Google. This information comes from Google Play and Android, not from Jacket, and is covered by Google’s privacy policy.

Deleting your data

Uninstalling Jacket deletes all of its data from your device, downloaded voices included. Backup files you exported, and any Android backup in your Google account, are not affected; you manage those yourself.

Children

Jacket is not directed at children under 13. It collects no personal information from anyone, including children.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted in the Jacket repository with a new effective date.

Contact

Questions about this policy: open an issue at github.com/friendlyglobal/jacket/issues. Issues are public, so please don’t include personal information.
